I've hired a lot of people. Onboarding checklists always sound boring in
the planning meeting — then week one hits and you realize nobody wrote
down where the staging credentials live.

The teams that get this right aren't the ones with the fanciest tool.
They're the ones who actually update the checklist after every hire — not
once a quarter, not "when we get around to it." Every single time.

I like a short list over a long one. Five real steps beat fifteen
aspirational ones. And honestly? Half the value is just having a place
where the new hire can ask "am I supposed to know this yet" without
feeling dumb.
