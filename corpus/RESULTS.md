### Round 1

Three measurements over the same 300-document corpus, same day, same corpus
sha256 set. Only `lib/ar-detector/lexicons.js` differs between them, and no
weight, threshold or gate in `index.js` was touched in any of them.

| # | change | FP rate (all) | Wilson 95% | mean score (all) | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | baseline: `lexicons.js` as it stood at the start of round 1 | 1.67% (5/300) | 0.71% to 3.84% | 10.92 | `docs/evidence/round1-fp-measure-baseline.txt` |
| 1 | IMP-12: add `AR-MSA-031` (light-verb calques, P2, minCount 2) and `AR-MSA-032` (collocation calques, P2, minCount 1) | 1.67% (5/300) | 0.71% to 3.84% | 10.95 | `docs/evidence/round1-fp-measure-imp12.txt` |
| 2 | IMP-17: `AR-SH-001` matches جدير بالذكر and جدير بالإشارة as stems, so the ال-prefixed and proclitic-plus-ال forms hit | 1.67% (5/300) | 0.71% to 3.84% | 10.97 | `docs/evidence/round1-fp-measure.txt` |

Reading of the deltas:

- **Neither change moved the false-positive rate at all.** The same five
  documents are flagged before and after, and no document crossed either
  threshold in either direction. The whole round-1 lexicon work is visible
  only as a 0.05-point rise in the mean score across 300 documents, which is
  a handful of P2 hits spread thinly.
- **IMP-12, `AR-MSA-031` gate.** The pattern was also measured at
  `minCount: 1` (mean 11.01, FP 1.67%, identical flag set) before settling
  on 2. The corpus says either gate is safe, so the conservative one was
  kept, because both the source and this reference entry frame the
  construction as a density tell and a single periphrasis as a legitimate
  stylistic choice.
- **IMP-17 raised no new false positive**, which is what the acceptance
  criterion asked. The widening is narrow by construction: it applies to two
  entries of one pattern, opt-in per entry through `stemPhrases`, and the
  default `phrases` behaviour is unchanged and separately tested.
- **No lexicon entry was removed or narrowed in this round.** The
  remediation branch in the IMP-01 acceptance criteria (list the
  top-flagged documents, fix clear lexicon over-fires, re-measure) was
  prepared for a rate above 2% with a Wilson upper bound above 5%. The
  measured rate is 1.67% with an upper bound of 3.84%, so no lexicon entry
  was touched on false-positive grounds. The top-flagged documents are
  listed above regardless, because they say something the rate does not.

### What the five flagged documents actually show

**Four of the five are a routing failure, not a lexicon failure.** The
documents scoring 83, 72, 71 and 68 are all ordinary MSA Wikipedia articles
that `lib/lang.js` routed to the **Egyptian** engine, after which they were
flagged for writing MSA: `AR-EGT-001` (MSA vocabulary in Egyptian-target
text) supplies 36 of their hits, with `AR-EGT-005`, `AR-EGT-002` and
`AR-EGT-026` (MSA leakage) on top. Nothing in the Egyptian lexicon is wrong
about them. They are being measured against a variety they were never
written in, which `scripts/README.md` already records as a known limitation
("Wrong-dialect text is not detected", and the dialect-ID note under "Known
limitations").

This is the single largest false-positive source the corpus exposes, it sits
in `lib/lang.js`'s variety selection rather than in any lexicon, and it is
out of scope for this pass. It is worth an improvement row of its own: an
Egyptian or Levantine verdict on a document whose dialect evidence is thin
should be rejected before the dialect lexicon ever runs, the same way
`detect.js`'s register-mix check already gates promotion in the other
direction.

**The fifth is `AR-MSA-006` (تم/يتم) on its own.** The document scoring 67
has exactly one issue id, `AR-MSA-006`, fifteen times. Across the whole
corpus that id fires in 45 of the 49 documents that reach `MIXED`, with 210
hits, far ahead of the next id (`AR-SH-007`, 16 documents, 39 hits). The
passive تم/يتم plus verbal noun is simply how Arabic encyclopedic prose
reports events whose agent is irrelevant (تم بناؤه عام كذا), so it is
extremely common in exactly this register.

No change was made for it, for two reasons. It is a documented critical
upstream pattern (`ar-msa.md` AR-MSA-006, and the critical-priority note at
the end of that file lists it among the three always-fix MSA patterns), so
removing the phrases would be a doctrine change, not a false-positive fix.
And the realistic remedy is not the phrase list at all: it is a density gate
(hits per hundred words) rather than the current flat `minCount: 2`, and
`minCount` semantics and the repeat-discount that turns 15 hits into a score
of 67 both live in `index.js`, which this pass does not own. Recorded here
as the measured evidence for whoever does own it: **AR-MSA-006 is the one
Arabic pattern this corpus shows accumulating to a false `AI` verdict on its
own.**

### Caveats on the number itself

- **Two registers, not a cross-section of Arabic.** Encyclopedic and news
  only. A `literary` register was planned and not reached (see
  `corpus/README.md`). Formal Arabic prose that is not encyclopedic, and any
  dialect writing at all, is unmeasured. The engine's dialect lexicons in
  particular have no false-positive measurement here, which matters because
  the routing failure above means they are what ordinary MSA sometimes gets
  measured against.
- **Every document is Wikimedia text.** Wikipedia register is its own thing,
  and the `random` class carries whatever machine-translated and
  bot-generated prose Arabic Wikipedia contains. That is why the provenance
  breakdown is reported: `random` flags at 3.00% against `featured` at
  0.00%, which is a statement about the sample.
- **Documents are trimmed to at most 600 Arabic words** at a paragraph
  boundary (the first paragraph is kept whole, so a long opening paragraph
  can exceed the cap; the observed maximum is 809 words). The engine applies
  no length normalization, so an untrimmed 10,000-word article would measure
  that missing normalization rather than the lexicon.
- **n is small where it matters most.** The `featured` class is 25
  documents, so its 0.00% carries a Wilson upper bound of 13.32%. Only the
  all-register figure has a usefully tight interval.
- **This is a false-positive measurement and nothing else.** It says nothing
  about whether the engine catches AI text. True-positive behaviour is
  covered only by this repository's own fixtures, which are synthetic.
